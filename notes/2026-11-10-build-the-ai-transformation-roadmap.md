## What good sequencing looks like
1. Map dependencies: identify the data, governance and process dependencies behind each candidate initiative
2. Assess absorption capacity: ask what the org can actually absorb in a given phase, not just what is technically ready
3. Place people work early: people and process work goes first, not last
4. Build a defensible sequence: produce a sequence whose logic a sceptical stakeholder can challenge on its merits

## Definitions
- AI transformation roadmap: a phased, milestone-driven plan that sequences an enterprise's AI investments from assessment through production and scale, tied to specific business outcomes. Unlike a project plan, it treats strategy, data readiness, governance and organisational design as interdependent workstreams rather than a task list.
- Organisational absorption: the organisation's capacity to actually adopt an initiative in a given phase (data pipelines, governance, change capacity, resolved integration dependencies). Once value and AI fit are established, absorption is usually the binding constraint that decides what belongs in a phase and what must wait.
- Momentum: the organisational confidence and executive commitment that a visible early win generates, which later, larger bets require. Sequencing for momentum means placing quick wins early so each funded phase creates the capital to reach the next: "sequence for momentum, not speed."
- The absorption question: can this organisation absorb this initiative in this phase? Checks four conditions: data pipelines in place, a governance structure to approve and monitor it, team change capacity to adopt it, integration dependencies resolved before the phase begins.
- The momentum test: a tiebreaker for uncertain placements. If this initiative succeeds in Phase One, does it create enough organisational confidence to fund Phase Two? If yes, Phase One; if it needs Phase-Two foundations first, it waits.

## Phase 1 or Phase 2?
Every candidate is checked against four conditions:
- Data pipeline: the flow of information the initiative needs is already connected and usable
- Governance: someone is authorised to approve, monitor and take responsibility for it
- Team capacity: the people who would run and manage it have the time and training to do so
- Integration dependency: is there a connection to an existing system or workflow that has to be built or confirmed first?

Each condition gets a quick read: green (ready), yellow (a gap, but manageable), red (not ready, a blocking dependency). Clears the bar -> Phase One (started now). Doesn't -> Phase Two, held back until the missing foundation is built. Unclear -> momentum test.

## Value / feasibility quadrants
- Priority now: high value, high feasibility. These advance first, no ceremony, no debate.
- Strategic bets: high value, lower feasibility. Worth the investment but need prerequisite work: data readiness, capability building, dependency resolution.
- Quick wins: lower value, high feasibility. Build confidence and momentum, but keep them time-boxed so they don't crowd out higher value work.
- Kill or defer: low on both. Stay out of the portfolio. High feasibility alone is never a reason to fund.

A matrix drawn from intuition is just advocacy with axes. Weighted scoring fixes that:
- Score each candidate on fixed criteria
- Weight each criterion to reflect what you value
- Take the weighted average
In regulated settings risk may weigh far heavier, or act as a gate outright.

## Clustering trap
2x2 (value vs feasibility): perceived winners (HV, HF); clustered proposals compete for attention; no clear priority, decisions stall; bias source: sponsors favour safe picks.
When everything is a priority, nothing is.
Related bias: central tendency. Scores bunch in the safe middle, avoiding the extremes that would force real decisions.

Facilitation habits that fix clustering:
- Set boundaries at the median
- Keep absolute gates
- Use the full range: push evaluators to spread scores, avoid the safe mid
- Demand evidence: any top-right candidate must show proof, not optimism

## From ranked list to portfolio
1. Quick wins: early, build credibility
2. Strategic bets: core functions, longer horizons
3. Moonshots: capabilities you don't have yet

Summarising:
- Step 1: assign quadrants
- Step 2: fine-rank high value
- Step 3: order by dependency

Underneath all of this, the core principle: value first. Let feasibility and risk shape sequence and prerequisites, not override value in selection.

## Value-Feasibility-Risk Sort
Question it answers: you have inherited a dozen pilots, the board wants 3. How do you decide?
- Normalize: put all candidates on a common canvas
- Agree criteria: weight by strategy and risk
- Score independently: discuss divergences, don't just agree
- Read the quadrants: tie each survivor to a KPI, baseline and target
- Defend the order: tell the CFO why each is first, second or parked
